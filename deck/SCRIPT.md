# SCRIPT.md — the spoken pitch, word for word

The words that go with `src/content/slides.ts`. Two presenters, Thai + English, nine
slides plus a four-minute demo. Rehearse from this; present from the deck.

- **TH** = the Thai presenter. **EN** = the English presenter.
- `→` = advance the deck (space, or the remote's PageDown).
- Lines in **bold quotes** are meant to be said close to verbatim. Everything else is a cue.
- **◇ marks Thai that was drafted here, not by the native speaker who corrected the
  slides.** Her wording on the slides is authoritative and appears unmarked below. Get her
  pass on every ◇ line before the first rehearsal — do not present them cold.

The rail adds up to **9:40 of speaking plus a 0:15 cover — 9:55**, not 9:00. That is the
deck's own budget, not a mistake in it. If the room is strict about nine minutes, cut in
this order and rehearse the cut version:

1. **Prep 2:00 → 1:15.** Do not read the VRAM table aloud; say one sentence and let them
   photograph it. (−45s)
2. **Business 1:00 → 0:50.** Name the three tiers, skip reading the payback rows. (−10s)

Never cut the demo. It is 45% of the time and it is the half that sells.

>

>
> ◇ **"และข้อสุดท้ายสำคัญที่สุด — MALI ส่งต่อข้อมูลการคัดกรองให้พยาบาล เพื่อประกอบการประเมินและดำเนินการต่อ"**
>
> ◇ **"พยาบาลเป็นผู้ตรวจทานทุกเคส AI ไม่เคยเป็นผู้ตัดสินสุดท้าย"**

That last line is the most load-bearing sentence in the first half. In a hospital room the
safety objection forms inside the first minute; this kills it before it hardens. Say it to
the clinical people, not to the slide.

Say "MALI" as a name, not as a product we bring in ("MALI คือ…", not "เราจึงพา MALI
เข้ามา…"). The slide was rewritten specifically to drop that framing.

`→`

## Demo — 4:00 · two scenarios, no more

The largest block in the pitch. Pre-flight is `docs/demo-runbook.md` §0 — pristine HIS
reseed, three tabs already logged in, ten-second mic check in the actual room. If audio
fails, play yesterday's screen recording and keep narrating over it; you lose nothing.

**Three things to verify on the running system before you say them:**

1. Whether the booth asks for an **HN** or a visit ID on the day — say the word the screen
   shows. Identity moved to HN on 2026-08-20; the runbook text still says VN.
2. Whether BP is actually **skipped** in Scenario A. That is a property of the complaint
   template, not the patient's age, and templates change. If it gets asked, rewrite beat 4
   to describe what actually happened.
3. Which patient fires the emergency in Scenario B. Run the run.

### Scenario A — the ordinary patient · ≈2:00 · TH speaks, EN drives

Runbook **Run 1** — Waraporn Srisuk (~33), routine → General OPD. The point of this
scenario is **ordinariness**. Say the word "ปกติ". Do not narrate architecture here.

| Beat | On screen | Say |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The booth greets by name | **"ระบบดึงข้อมูลจาก HIS ของโรงพยาบาลเอง — ไม่ต้องกรอกอะไรใหม่"** |
| 2 | Spoken identity confirmation | **"ยืนยันตัวตนก่อนเสมอ"** |
| 3 | Patient speaks; MALI asks follow-ups aloud; chips visible | **"ตอบด้วยเสียงหรือแตะก็ได้"** |

### Scenario B — the catch · ≈2:00 · EN speaks, TH drives

Runbook **Run 3** — ประเสริฐ สุขสม (~78), measured BP → **Emergency**.

**B1, the catch (≈1:00).** Elderly patient, the complaint sounds mild, the cuff reads
dangerously high, and the interview ends there.

> **"The complaint sounds mild. The cuff does not agree."**
>
> **"The objective reading is merged into the state *before* the red-flag gate — so a
> dangerous vital ends the interview on turn one, no matter how the conversation was
> going."**
>
> **"That is the patient the front desk would have asked to take a seat."**

**B2, the action and the audit (≈1:00).** Switch to the nurse tab.

> **"The case is already waiting for a nurse — transcript, level, the vitals, the
> recommended department."**
>
> **"And the reasoning, with citations back to your own triage manual. Not a model's
> opinion — the rule that fired, and the page it came from."**
>
> **"The nurse confirms, and Stage 2 writes the destination and an SBAR handover into the
> record and returns a queue number. Stage 1 — the booth's own measurements — wrote itself
> the moment the patient stopped talking."**

The closing sentence of the whole demo, then stop talking:

> **"The model reads language. The decision is made by deterministic, versioned rules
> encoded from your own manual — same input, same answer, every time, and every decision
> traces to the rule that fired."**

**Do not** tour the criteria editor, the AI metrics dashboard, disease surveillance, the
desktop widget or database settings. Each has a one-line answer ready in Q&A. Do not add a
third scenario, even if the demo ran fast.

## 5 · Business model — 1:00 · EN, with one TH sentence

On screen: three tiers, then the business case and the payback rows.

> **"Three ways to deploy MALI — and the software and the service carry the value.
> Hardware is just how it gets delivered."**
>
> **"Pilot: 75,000 baht fixed, three months, one station, and it ends with a measured
> impact report. It is credited toward deployment."**
>
> **"Deploy is the main model: 180,000 one-time per station, then 120,000 a year for the
> licence — about 540,000 over three years."**
>
> **"Or MALI as a service: 16,000 a month per station, no upfront capital. Over three years
> that is 576,000 — thirty-six thousand more than buying it."**
>
> **"At a target of 150 patients a day, one station is designed to release about 1,300
> nurse-hours a year. At 250 baht an hour, that is around 325,000 baht of capacity, against
> a first-year cost of 300,000."**

Then the sentence that makes the slide credible instead of salesy. Do not let it drop:

> **"Released capacity is not cash. Only the share the hospital actually converts — less
> overtime, a post you did not need to fill — is a real saving. That is why payback is a
> range rather than a promise, and it is exactly what the pilot measures."**

If they engage with the payback rows, give the shape, not all four: **"Fully converted, about
eleven months; at half, about twenty-two."**

TH, one sentence, on the medical devices:

> ◇ **"อุปกรณ์ทางการแพทย์เป็นของผู้ผลิตที่ได้รับการรับรอง เราเป็นผู้เชื่อมต่อ ไม่ได้ผลิตเอง"**

## 6 · Pilot success criteria — 0:40 · EN

On screen: nine KPIs, baseline and measured.

> **"A pilot should prove value, not just prove that it works."**
>
> **"So we establish a baseline before MALI arrives, then measure the same nine metrics
> throughout. Both halves, every metric — so nothing gets chosen after the results are in."**
>
> **"Patients screened, screening time, nurse minutes per patient, waiting time,
> interventions, completion rate, both satisfaction scores, and the escalation and error
> rate."**
>
> **"At the end you get a Hospital Operational Impact Report, and that report is the
> foundation of the deployment proposal — including whether there should be one."**

Say the deck's own note out loud somewhere in there: **a demo shows it works; this measures
whether it was worth doing.**

`→`

## 7 · Deployment prep — 2:00 · EN leads, TH takes the hospital-side items

On screen: three columns — Technical, Data and integration, Hardware.

> **"Everything runs on your hardware. Three API calls are all that crosses the boundary."**

**Technical (≈0:35).** Lead with the honest item, in these words:

> **"Sign-in today is bearer tokens and three roles. Single sign-on against your directory
> is not built yet — and we need four answers to build it: SAML or OIDC, which identity
> provider you run, which staff groups map to admin, nurse and viewer, and how long a
> session should stay valid."**
>
> **"The booth sits on your LAN. No public internet. Cloud egress exists only while we run
> cloud inference, and the on-premise build removes it."**
>
> **"The one dependency that gates the pilot start date is a HIS test endpoint and test
> records. We would like to agree that today."**

Ask for it in the room. Do not leave it as a bullet someone reads later.

**Data and integration (≈0:40).**

> **"Three operations. One read: patient by HN — name, birthdate, current visit,
> appointment flag, existing vitals. Two writes: Stage 1, the booth's measurements; Stage
> 2, the nurse-confirmed destination and SBAR."**
>
> **"We never connect to your database. You expose a small API, you choose the fields, and
> you can revoke us without touching it."**
>
> **"From you we need the triage manual as a PDF, your department list verbatim in Thai —
> eleven are routable today, about 64% of routed encounters — a sample prescreen export,
> and a named clinical owner who signs off criteria versions."**

TH takes anything hospital-side that lands here — the manual, department names, who signs
off — in Thai, then hands straight back.

**Hardware (≈0:45).** This is the slide procurement photographs. Point at it, do not read it.

> **"One RTX 4000 SFF Ada, twenty gigabytes. The Thai LLM, speech-to-text and
> text-to-speech together come to about fourteen of the twenty — the whole local voice
> stack fits on one card with room to spare."**
>
> **"The application server is modest: two vCPU, eight gigabytes of RAM, a hundred
> gigabytes of disk — and that same disk covers sessions, transcripts, the audit trail and
> the manual embeddings. The retention window is your call."**
>
> **"Per booth: touch screen, mic and speaker, a Bluetooth BP cuff, a thermometer and a
> slip printer."**
>
> **"This table is why on-premise is an engineering statement rather than an aspiration.
> Please photograph it."**

## Questions — off the clock · both

**Make the ask out loud before opening the floor. It is deliberately not on a slide.**

> **"What we are asking for is a pilot at OPD, a HIS test endpoint, the triage manual, and
> a named clinical owner. What you get back is the measured baseline."**
>
> **"Questions in either language — [TH name] takes the clinical and hospital-side ones, I
> take the technical."**

Answer in the language the question was asked in. The prepared answers are on `Q` in the
deck and in `src/content/qa.ts`. Two rules:

- **"What if the AI is wrong?"** → the AI does not decide. Say the sentence, then stop
  talking.
- **"What if the network or the HIS is down?"** → **verify the real behaviour on the
  running system before the pitch.** That answer is deliberately unfinished in the deck.
  Do not improvise it.
